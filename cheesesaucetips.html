Cheese Sauce Insights & Tips

Everything learned about smooth, silky cheese sauces — from technique to troubleshooting and personal observations spanning decades.

Print option available at the bottom of the page.

Technique Tips

  • Make a roux first (butter + flour) to stabilise the sauce.
  • Add milk slowly while whisking to prevent lumps.
  • Add cheese off heat to prevent graininess or oily separation.
  • Gradually mix and rewarm gently only if necessary.

Cheese Selection

  • Cheddar — classic melt and flavour
  • Gruyère — smooth, nutty, traditional French depth
  • Parmesan — seasoning boost, sharper flavour
  • Mozzarella — stringy, less ideal for a smooth sauce
  • Australian Tasty / mild cheddar — closest to Mainland Tasty from the 90s

Why This Works

  • The roux stabilises the dairy, preventing separation.
  • Slow milk addition prevents lumps.
  • Off-heat cheese melt keeps the sauce silky.
  • Gradual mixing stops splitting or graininess.

Common Problems & Fixes

  • Lumps — milk added too fast. Fix: whisk hard or use a stick blender.
  • Grainy cheese — cheese overheated. Fix: add cheese off heat, use a gentler-melting cheese.
  • Too thick — roux is strong. Fix: add more milk gradually.
  • Too thin — sauce hasn't thickened. Fix: simmer gently to allow starch to thicken.

Block vs Pre-Shredded Cheese

Shred block cheese yourself instead of using pre-shredded. That little grainy coating on pre-shredded cheese? It took about 26 years to figure out what it was.

Pre-shredded cheese contains anti-caking agents. They prevent sticking but also subtly affect flavour and texture:

  • Can make sauce slightly grainy
  • Changes melting behaviour and mouthfeel
  • Flavour is slightly muted compared to block cheese

Block cheese gives a smoother texture, better control, and more authentic taste — the same reason it's the standard in a professional kitchen.
