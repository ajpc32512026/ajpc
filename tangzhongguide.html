Tangzhong (Water Roux) Guide

A complete guide to mastering the Tangzhong method — including scaling, flour alternatives, and various cooking techniques for the softest possible bread.

Print option available at the bottom of the page.

1. The Basic Ratio

  • Flour: 30 g wheat flour (taken from total dough flour)
  • Liquid: 190 g water (accounts for evaporation during cooking)

Ratio: Roughly 1 part flour to 6 parts water (wetter style).

Storage

Cool completely and store airtight in the fridge for up to 3–4 days. Always bring to room temperature before adding to your dough.

2. Scaling Tangzhong

  • Quadruple batch: 120 g flour + 760 g water → yields ~880 g
  • Half batch: 15 g flour + 95 g water → yields ~110 g

Pro Tip: For extra-soft bread, you can increase the roux to 1.5× (e.g., 50 g flour + 315 g water), but ensure you reduce the liquid in the main dough accordingly.

3. Corn Flour Variation

Corn flour thickens faster than wheat and provides a custard-like crumb, though it offers no gluten structure.

  • Wheat version: 30 g flour + 190 g water
  • Corn version: 10–12 g corn flour + 190 g water

Hybrid Option: Use 15 g wheat and 6 g corn flour for the best balance of softness and structure.

4. Gluten-Free Tangzhong

For gluten-free baking, a roux is essential for moisture retention. A blend of starches works best:

  • Rice Flour (40–50%): High starch, provides the bulk of the thickening.
  • Buckwheat Flour (30–40%): Adds earthy flavour and deeper colour.
  • Tapioca Starch (10–20%): Adds essential chewiness (do not exceed 20% to avoid gumminess).

Example GF Batch

15 g Buckwheat + 15 g Rice Flour + 5 g Tapioca Starch + 190 g Water.

5. Cooking Methods

Stove Method (Recommended)

Whisk flour and water in a small saucepan. Cook over low-medium heat, stirring constantly until the mixture thickens to a pudding consistency (approx. 65°C / 150°F).

Microwave Method

Mix ingredients in a microwave-safe bowl. Heat at medium power in 20–30 second bursts, stirring well between each interval until the whisk leaves lines in the paste.

Electric Frypan Method

Ideal for large batches. Combine and stir constantly over low heat to avoid sticking. Stop just before the paste becomes stiff.

Warning

If the Tangzhong becomes very stiff or gluey, it is overcooked. It is safer to pull it off the heat when it is slightly thinner than desired, as it will continue to thicken while cooling.
