🍞 Tangzhong Guide

A complete guide to making Tangzhong, scaling it, adjusting the roux, using corn flour, and gluten-free alternatives. Includes methods for stove, electric frypan, and microwave.

1. Basic Tangzhong

  • 30 g wheat flour (taken from total dough flour)
  • 190 g water (accounts for evaporation during cooking)

Ratio: roughly 1 part flour : 6 parts water (wetter style).

Storage: Cool completely, store airtight in fridge for 3–4 days. Bring to room temperature before adding to dough.

2. Scaling Tangzhong

  • Quadruple batch: 120 g flour + 760 g water → yields ~880 g Tangzhong (less than 1 liter)
  • Half batch: 15 g flour + 95 g water → yields ~110 g Tangzhong

3. Increasing Roux for Softer Bread

  • Example 1.5× roux: 50 g flour + 315 g water

Tip: Reduce extra water or add a little flour in the main dough to keep it manageable.

4. Using Corn Flour Instead of Wheat Flour

  • Thickens faster than wheat flour
  • No gluten contribution — structure depends on main dough flour
  • Gives a custard-like crumb

Conversion Example:

  • Original wheat Tangzhong: 30 g flour + 190 g water
  • Corn flour version: 10–12 g corn flour + 190 g water

Cook gently until thickened, then cool before adding to dough.

Hybrid Option:

Combine half wheat, half corn flour for extra softness without gumminess.

5. Gluten-Free Tangzhong

Using buckwheat, rice flour, and tapioca starch:

  • Buckwheat Flour: ~30–40% of flour, adds flavor and color, heavier texture
  • Rice Flour: ~40–50%, high starch, thickens well
  • Tapioca Starch: ~10–20%, very starchy, adds chewiness (too much → gummy)

Example Batch:

  • 15 g buckwheat flour
  • 15 g rice flour
  • 5 g tapioca starch
  • 190 g water

Cook gently until thickened, stir constantly, then cool before adding to dough.

Tip: Use binders like xanthan gum or psyllium husk in the main dough; Tangzhong improves moisture but won't provide elasticity.

6. Cooking Methods for Tangzhong

Stove Method (Preferred)

  • Mix flour and water in a small saucepan.
  • Cook over low-medium heat, stirring constantly.
  • Remove when it thickly coats the back of a spoon but is still pourable.
  • Cool before adding to dough.

Electric Frypan Method

  • Use a small frypan with temperature control if possible.
  • Combine flour and water, stir constantly to avoid sticking.
  • Heat until lightly thickened; stop just before it becomes stiff.
  • Cool before adding to dough.

Microwave Method

  • Mix flour and water in a microwave-safe bowl.
  • Microwave at medium power for 20–30 seconds at a time, stirring after each interval.
  • Repeat until it coats the back of a spoon but is still pourable.
  • Cool completely before adding to dough.

Overcooking Warning: Tangzhong that becomes very thick, stiff, or gluey has been overcooked. Slightly thinner than desired is safer; it will thicken as it cools.

7. Quick Reference: Tangzhong Amounts

Approximate Tangzhong amounts for 1 standard loaf (~500 g flour) depending on flour type:

Flour Type Flour in Tangzhong Water in Tangzhong Notes
Wheat Flour 30 g 190 g Classic Tangzhong; covers ~1 loaf
Wheat + Corn Flour Hybrid 15 g wheat + 10 g corn 190 g Gives custardy, soft crumb
Gluten-Free (Buckwheat + Rice + Tapioca) 15 g buckwheat + 15 g rice + 5 g tapioca 190 g Use binders in dough; keeps crumb moist
1.5× Larger Loaf (Wheat) 50 g 315 g Extra roux for softer bread

Tip: Always cool Tangzhong before adding to dough; adjust water slightly if making smaller or larger batches.
