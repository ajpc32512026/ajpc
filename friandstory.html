The Humble Friand

Why I got out of bed at 2AM — because some disasters won't let you sleep until you get them right.

Print option available at the bottom of the page.

Baking is often called a science — all precision, calm, and tidy measurements. In my kitchen, though, it's mostly stubborn willpower and questionable choices. The kind that leave you barefoot on icy tiles at dawn, muttering threats at a bag of almond meal that cost more than a week of groceries.

The oven hums like an old accomplice, full of secrets it refuses to share. Somewhere between sifting and swearing, you start to wonder why the universe hates you, and whether anyone truly needs another macaron.

But you keep going. Because in the quiet moments — between the soft thump of a sponge cake collapsing and the rhythmic scrape of a whisk — you remember: this isn't just baking. It's ritual. It's memory. It's a tiny bit of magic disguised as sugar and heat.

🥄 The Disaster Begins

This is the tale of how I demolished my first batch of friands, considered retiring from baking, and somehow mastered them with nothing more than a plastic jug and sheer stubbornness.

It began like most culinary catastrophes: butter barely softened, eggs fridge-cold, and I misread the recipe so badly it felt like assembling flat-pack furniture without diagrams.

At one point, I confused icing sugar with flour. Twice. By then, I was too deep to turn back. A bowl full of potential chaos and a point to prove — mainly to myself.

❄️ First Attempt: Muffin Tin Mayhem

All ingredients laid out — butter, sugar, almond meal. Wallet cried. Result? Garbage. Angry cupcakes tasting like disappointment dusted in powdered sugar. Batter overflowed. Middles sank. Even the muffin tin seemed ashamed.

Batter sat too long, butter split, hopes curdled. Out came regret — a crusty brick with a buttery puddle underneath. Poked it once. It didn't fight back. Just slumped further.

By 9PM I was cold, tired, and deeply insulted by twelve tiny cakes. Cleaned up, crawled into bed. Sleep refused. An idea nudged me awake — a "What if…" that refused to wait for morning.

☕ The 2:30AM Redemption Bake

I got up. No lights. No music. Just winter air biting my ankles and a score to settle. Fired up the oven — 180°C, no hesitation. Dry ingredients in one bowl. Wet in another.

Revelation: Don't mix wet and dry until ready to pour. At 2:30AM, it felt like divine intervention.

2-litre plastic jug — the hero. Folded dry into wet with gentleness. No whisking. No rage. Quiet determination. Smooth pour into molds. Straight into the oven — like I'd been doing this all my life.

🎉 BOOM. Success.

Golden, light, moist, little orange–poppyseed clouds of redemption. Wipes out earlier disasters and makes you feel like a kitchen prodigy. Fragile. Glorious. Slightly uneven — perfect in the way only hard-won triumphs are.

The kitchen smelled of citrus, sugar, and victory. This batch would stick with me for years.

🧠 The Moral

You're going to wreck things in the kitchen. Burn them. Sink them. Split them. But drag yourself up — even at 2:30AM in winter — and try again. That's when you become a cook. Not the apron. Not the shiny toys. Just that stubborn whisper:

"One more go."

Sometimes all it takes is a bowl, a jug, and a bit of heart. The best bakes? Born after the worst ones. If you walk away after failure, why bother starting at all?
